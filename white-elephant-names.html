Every Name for the White Elephant Gift Exchange

One game, at least twenty-five names. A regional, historical, and frankly unnecessary number of aliases for the most beloved stealing game in America.

A.K.A. everything

Quick answer

The white elephant gift exchange has at least 25 names because the game spread by word of mouth and each region named it for the feature it liked best: swapping (Yankee Swap), stealing (Dirty Santa, Thieving Elves), dice (Rob Your Neighbor), or the gift pile (white elephant). The basic game is the same everywhere.

Why so many names?

The game spread by word of mouth — family to family, break room to break room — and every region named it after the feature it liked best: the swapping (Yankee Swap), the stealing (Dirty Santa, Thieving Elves), the dice (Rob Your Neighbor), or the gift pile itself (white elephant). Same skeleton everywhere: bring a wrapped gift, draw numbers, unwrap or steal, freeze at three. Here's the full map.

Which name should your invitation use?

Use the name your room already knows — it sets expectations better than any rules paragraph. Say "Yankee Swap" and New Englanders bring gifts worth stealing; say "Bad Santa" and they'll bring a half-used candle with a scratcher taped inside (a real and glorious submission from the field). Say "white elephant" anywhere and almost everyone knows the shape of the night. For rules that work under any alias, head to the rulebook.

Names FAQ

What's another name for a white elephant gift exchange?

Yankee Swap (New England), Dirty Santa (the South), Pollyanna (PA/NJ, where it often means a Secret Santa–style draw), and Rob Your Neighbor (Midwest) are the big four. Dozens more circulate: Thieving Elves, Shifty Santa, the Grinch Game, Cutthroat Christmas, Steal-a-Thon, and friends.

Are all these names the same game?

Mechanically yes — numbered turns, unwrap or steal, freeze rules. The names differ mainly by region and gift-pool expectations.

What is "Pollyanna"? I thought that was a movie.

Pollyanna (1913 novel, then films) is also a gift-exchange name in parts of Pennsylvania and New Jersey, but usage is disputed. Some groups use it for the white elephant stealing game; others mean a Secret Santa–style draw with no stealing. Ask your group which game it means.

What's the Left-Right game?

A different mechanic where gifts pass around a circle as a story is read aloud with "left" and "right" cues — no stealing at all, but hosts often use the name interchangeably for casual swaps.
