How a premium gift warps the game

Money changes the math. Run it on purpose, not by accident.

Under the Three-Owner Rule, a luxury lip balm will hit its three owners in the first three turns — it is the de facto trophy, so the Master of Ceremonies should call it out loud when it unwraps. If the pile splits cheap-gag versus premium, consider announcing a house amendment before Player 1 goes: a premium-tier second swap, or a no-touch first pass for anything over $50. In a Yankee Swap, premium gifts are exactly why the final-swap rule exists — the last player should get one honest shot at the French press.

Premium gift FAQ

The questions that come up when someone puts a luxury lip balm on the table.

How much should a premium white elephant gift cost?

Most parties set a $20–$25 limit; $30–$100 is the generous tier. If you are spending that much, you are either independently wealthy or you really love this group — both are legal, but announce the budget break to the host first.

Is it fair to bring an expensive gift to a white elephant?

It is allowed unless your host caps the price. Expect it to be stolen to its three-owner limit immediately, which is either the point or the problem.

What is the best $30–$100 white elephant gift?

A steal magnet with broad appeal: the Frieling French press for caffeine people, Magical Athlete for game people, a wooden Artifact puzzle for quiet people, and La Mer lip balm for anyone who wants to watch the room react.

Should expensive gifts get their own rules?

Some houses add a premium tier — a second swap round or a no-touch first pass. Hand the call to your Master of Ceremonies and announce it before Player 1 unwraps.
