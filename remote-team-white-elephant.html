Remote Team White Elephant

Your team spans four time zones, three contractors, and one person who only attends meetings from a kayak. Stolen Elephants turns that spread into an advantage: a white elephant that fits inside everyone's actual workday, with the steal chain doing the team building.

No Conference Room Required

Quick answer

A remote team can run a white elephant over a week instead of a single meeting. The host posts one event link, each player is pinged when it's their turn, and unwraps and steals happen between meetings, about fifteen minutes per person. Pick the budget first; $20 is the office standard.

The pitch to your people team: team building without the trust falls — and with double the laughter. Nobody has to disclose a childhood fear on a whiteboard; they just have to defend a desk waffle maker from a colleague two oceans away. No gift yet? No panic. When you add your gift, just paste a link from an online retailer — Amazon, Target, or anywhere else — and the app turns it into a proper pile entry. Your recipient gets a direct link to their winning loot, not a mystery box they have to guess about.

A week-long game that fits the workday

Fifteen minutes per person, spread across the week. Zero calendar invites.

Monday: open the pile

DAY 1

The host posts the event link in Slack. Players peek at nothing — the pile is sealed — and get their draw order.

Set the budget cap ($20 is the office standard), paste the link, done. Even the async-averse can handle it in a coffee break.

Why distributed teams get more out of it than co-located ones

The gift pile becomes the water cooler.

Co-located offices have the break room; remote teams have notifications. An async white elephant manufactures the small, harmless drama — who stole the good candles? — that distributed teams otherwise never get. It works for hybrid squads too: the folks in the conference room and the folks on the screen see the same board, and nobody in either group is a second-class player.

It also scales with the org chart: a team of 8 plays free, a department of 40 runs $19 per event, and a whole-company brawl up to 150 players is $99 — the same pricing as every Stolen Elephants event.

Keep it classy enough for the all-hands

A quick word from your friendly HR partner.

The office edition has its own etiquette — budget caps, no procedural-gift recycling, and a dedicated playbook. For a distributed team, add two: announce the house rules in writing before Monday, and let the app's MC functions make the controversial calls, because 'the software decided' ends debates that a manager can't.

Remote Team White Elephant FAQ

The questions that come up before the first steal.

How do you do a white elephant with a remote team?

Create an async event, share one join link, and let each person take their turn during their own workday. The app tracks turn order, steals, and locks — no meeting required, though an optional finale call is fun.

How long should an async work white elephant run?

Three to five business days is the sweet spot: enough time for every player to take a turn without being chased, short enough that the pile still feels alive on finale day.

What price cap works for a remote team gift exchange?

$20 is the office norm; some teams do $30 for a 'premium' year. Announce the cap with the invite so nobody shows up with a $95 lip balm against a $20 pile — or everyone does, and the steal chain gets legendary.

Is a virtual gift exchange good team building for hybrid teams?

Yes — in-room and remote players share one board, one order, and one steal chain, so neither group becomes the second-class audience of a conference-room-only party.
