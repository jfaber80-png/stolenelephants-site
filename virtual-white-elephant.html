Virtual White Elephant & Yankee Swap

The rules of the room, without the room. Stolen Elephants runs the draw, the steal chains, and the Three-Owner Rule for games played live on video, stretched across a week, or followed from a hospital bed — nobody gets handed a spectator badge.

Live · Async · Spectate

Quick answer

A virtual white elephant uses the same rules online: players draw numbers, unwrap or steal, and the Three-Owner Rule locks gifts. You can play live on video, spread turns across a week, or follow along from anywhere. Stolen Elephants holds the turn order and tracks every steal and lock automatically.

One pile, three speeds: play live on a video call with the app keeping the order, play async so each teammate takes their turn inside their own workday, or flip on spectator-plus so anyone following along can still be pulled into the steal chain. Same gifts, same drama, zero conference-room booking. No gift yet? No panic. When you add your gift, just paste a link from an online retailer — Amazon, Target, or anywhere else — and the app turns it into a proper pile entry. Your recipient gets a direct link to their winning loot, not a mystery box they have to guess about.

How a virtual game runs, start to finish

Five steps. The host does about ten minutes of work total.

  1. Create the event — set the mode (live, async, or hybrid), the price cap, and any house amendments.
  2. Invite the link — one URL joins from phone or laptop, no app store detour. Dispersed team, distant cousins: same link.
  3. The app draws numbers — order is public and final, which kills 90% of pre-game conspiracy theories.
  4. Turns fire in sequence — unwrap or steal; the software enforces the Three-Owner Rule, blocks immediate steal-backs, and runs the final swap automatically.
  5. Everyone leaves with a gift — and the receipts to prove whose Grinch hands touched it last.

The rules don't change — the room does

Software referees better than a cousin with a beer.

A virtual game is still a white elephant: three owners and a gift locks, no immediate steal-backs, and Player 1's final swap when the last gift opens. The difference is that the app — not a Master of Ceremonies — tracks every lock, which is exactly what you want when half the room is on mute. Async games get a short turn window per player so one distracted uncle can't stall the chain across a long weekend.

Virtual White Elephant FAQ

The questions that come up before the first steal.

Can you play white elephant virtually with people in different time zones?

Yes. Run it async: the event stays open across days, each player takes their turn when their day allows, and the app enforces the same Three-Owner Rule and steal-back bans as a live game.

How does an async white elephant work?

Players are notified when it's their turn, unwrap or steal from the shared pile, and the next player is prompted automatically. A week-long game fits every team's calendar without a single meeting invite.

Can someone join a white elephant from a hospital or airport?

Yes — spectator-plus mode lets anyone follow the action live and take real turns from a phone browser. Remote players are participants, not afterthoughts.

Do virtual games work for Yankee Swap too?

Yes. The Yankee Swap final-swap sequence runs the same way: the app tracks owners and executes the last-player swap without a vote count on a whiteboard.
