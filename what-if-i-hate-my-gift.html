What If I Hate My Gift?

It happens — almost every game, somebody unwraps Uncle Randall’s Dollar Store find from 2016 and has to smile through it. Here are five real-world fixes that end with no hard feelings, because one person’s trash is another person’s treasure (and vice versa).

No Hard Feelings · Really

Quick answer

If you hate your white elephant gift, you have five options, from kindest to boldest. The gentlest is asking the giver if they'd like to donate it instead; most people are relieved, not offended. Others include passing it to a relative who liked it and offering to buy the item yourself.

First, the truth: this happens to one person almost every game. Somebody ends up with Uncle Randall’s Dollar Store find from 2016, the candle that smells like a dentist’s office, or the mug that says #1 Boss in a font from a gas station. The game’s structure already softens the blow — the Three-Owner Rule caps how many times you can be victimized, and Player 1’s final swap exists partly for this moment. What follows is what to do when it still lands on you.

Five fixes, from kindest to boldest

Real-world options. Pick the one that matches your family’s temperature.

Ask the giver to donate it

PHYSICAL GIFT

The kindest sentence in the sport: 'Would you mind if we donated it?' Nine givers out of ten say yes before you finish the question.

If it's a physical gift and you genuinely won't use it, simply ask the giver if they'd like to donate it somewhere instead — most people are relieved, not offended. Bonus route: send it to Aunt Edna, because she seemed to really like it when it was revealed. One person's trash is another person's treasure, and Edna has already claimed this one.

The Venmo redirect

UNORDERED GIFT

'Hey, Venmo me and I'll buy it here.' Five words that turn a dud into exactly what you wanted, at the same price, with zero feelings harmed.

If the gift could be ordered from a retailer and hasn't been purchased yet, the recipient can say: 'Hey, Venmo me and I'll buy it here' — same budget, your choice of color, size, or model. Works best when the game is async or the order hasn't gone in yet; check the app's end-of-game ordering so the redirect lands before the retailer ships.

The charity redirect

GIVE-WHERE

'Could you donate what you would have spent to the following charity?' Suddenly the worst gift of the night is someone's best news of the week.

Same setup as the Venmo redirect, different destination: 'Could you donate what you would have spent to the following charity?' You name the cause, they still gave a gift, and nobody's budget changes. It's the classiest steal-back in the modern game — the gift becomes a donation in everyone's name.

Regift it next year

LEGEND RETURNS

Every family has the travelling fruitcake. Under the Legend Returns Clause, yours can become next year's most feared unwrap.

Formally regift: keep it wrapped (or re-wrap it), store it with the decorations, and deploy it under the Legend Returns Clause next year. House rules govern — some families require the regift to change houses, which keeps the story alive and the target fair.

Trade quietly after the game

SIDE DEAL

Half the great trades in history happen in the driveway. Find the person who wanted your dud and swap — the app's ownership log makes it official.

After the final swap, find the one person who said 'wait, I actually wanted that' and trade. The game record shows the official winners; the driveway trade is between friends. This is also where 'one person's trash is another person's treasure' does its real work — the pile sorts people perfectly.

The spirit of the thing

Why none of these moves counts as an insult.

The white elephant deal has always been: one wrapped surprise, zero vetting, and the pile decides. Everyone agrees to that when they draw a number — so being disappointed isn’t a betrayal, and fixing it quietly isn’t a rejection. One person’s trash is another person’s treasure, and vice versa. The Aunt Edna option exists because she genuinely wanted that ceramic lighthouse; the charity redirect exists because the budget was already given. None of the five fixes costs the giver pride or the recipient money — and done with a smile, there are never hard feelings. (And if you gave the dud: laugh, take the Venmo, and enjoy your new status as a cautionary tale. It’s the cheapest tuition in the game.)

Virtual games make this even easier: with order-at-end-of-game, a dud link can be redirected before the retailer ever ships — the Venmo and charity options were practically invented for it.

“What if I hate my gift?” FAQ

The consolation-prize questions, answered with grace.

What do I do if I hate my white elephant gift?

It happens to one player almost every game — and there are gracious fixes: ask the giver to donate it, redirect an unopened order ('Venmo me and I'll buy it here'), redirect the spend to a charity, regift it next year, or trade quietly after the game. There are never hard feelings; one person's trash is another person's treasure.

Is it rude to ask the gift giver to donate my gift?

Not if you ask kindly and privately. Most givers would rather their gift find a good home than gather dust. Sending it to someone who loved it at the reveal — an Aunt Edna type — is the warmest version of the move.

Can I ask for money instead of a white elephant gift?

Only for un-ordered retailer gifts, and only gently: 'Hey, Venmo me and I'll buy it here' keeps the budget identical and lets the recipient pick the variant they actually want. Never demand cash for an already-shipped gift — donate the item or regift instead.

How do I regift a white elephant gift without being rude?

Follow the Legend Returns Clause: re-wrap it well, store it with the holiday decorations, and redeploy it next year — ideally to a different household so the story keeps traveling. The best regifts become family legends.

Does someone always end up unhappy with their gift?

Almost every game has one person who wishes they weren't stuck with Uncle Randall's Dollar Store find from 2016. The Three-Owner Rule caps the damage, the final swap gives Player 1 a fix, and the gracious fixes above handle the rest.
