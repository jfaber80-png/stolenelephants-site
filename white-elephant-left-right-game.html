The Left-Right Gift Exchange Game

A story gets read aloud. Every "left" and "right" whips every gift one seat around the circle. When the story ends, keep what you're holding. It's the white elephant's gentler cousin — and the only gift game a room of seven-year-olds can survive.

Pass it · don't steal it

Quick answer

The Left-Right game replaces white elephant's open-or-steal choice with a story. Everyone holds a wrapped gift while a host reads a story full of the words "left" and "right"; each time you hear one, you pass your gift one seat that way. When the story ends, you keep what you're holding. There is no stealing.

What is the Left-Right game?

The Left-Right game (also called the Left-Right Christmas game, Right-Left game, or a Pass-the-Parcel-style gift exchange) replaces the white elephant's steal-or-open choice with pure chaos-by-narration. Everyone holds a wrapped gift while the host reads a story peppered with the words left and right. Hear a direction word, pass your gift that way — one seat. No choices, no strategy, no stealing, and the story does all the work. Because nobody's gift is ever "taken," it's the exchange of choice for classrooms, church groups, and any room where tears would otherwise be on the agenda. Hosts often group it with the white elephant family of games — see how it compares on the all-names glossary.

Rules in four steps

  1. Everyone brings a wrapped gift

    One per guest, budget announced in the invite ($15–$25 typical). Gifts are anonymous and identical-looking — wrap everything in similar paper so nobody can game the pile.

  2. Circle up, gift in hand

    Everyone holds their own gift. Works from about 5 players to 60+; no numbers, no turn order, no waiting.

  3. Read the story aloud

    Every LEFT passes gifts one seat left; every RIGHT passes one seat right. Read slowly at first, then absolutely not slowly at all — pacing is the host's comedy instrument.

  4. Keep what you're holding

    When the story ends, the gift in your hands is yours. Everyone unwraps at once — the loudest moment of the party.

Two free read-aloud stories

Both are original — print them, or read from a phone. Direction cues are highlighted; aim for roughly 30 passes per story, which lands most people holding a gift they didn't bring.

Story 1: "The Wright Family's Left-Turn Christmas" (family/classic)

It was the night before Christmas, and Mrs. Wright was heading RIGHT out the door to finish her shopping. "If I don't go LEFT toward the mall RIGHT now, everything good will be gone!" she called, jumping in the car and turning LEFT out of the driveway. But she'd LEFT her wallet on the kitchen counter, so around she turned — RIGHT back home — only to find Mr. Wright standing LEFT holding a list twice as long. "No wonder you were headed LEFT out of here in a hurry," he said, "you LEFT this behind." So they went RIGHT to the mall together, turned LEFT past the toy store, went RIGHT into the gift shop, and walked out with presents for everyone LEFT on the list. That night, gifts were piled RIGHT under the tree — some slightly LEFT-over from last year — and the whole Wright family agreed: the best gifts are the ones you almost LEFT behind. Keep whatever you're holding RIGHT now, because this story is almost LEFT for done — and there's the RIGHT ending!

Story 2: "Lefty the White Elephant" (kids/short)

Lefty was a little elephant who always turned LEFT when all the other elephants turned RIGHT. One snowy night, Lefty found a mysterious gift sitting LEFT of the big oak tree. He carried it RIGHT home to show his friends, but they'd all gone LEFT to the river. So Lefty turned RIGHT around and followed them — LEFT at the pond, RIGHT over the hill, LEFT past the berry bushes — until he found the whole herd. They opened the gift together: it was a map! Two steps LEFT, three steps RIGHT, one more step LEFT… and there, RIGHT where the X was, they found a whole field of holiday treats. The herd cheered, and even the elephants who always went RIGHT agreed that going LEFT with friends was the RIGHT thing to do. And the gift in your hands RIGHT now? It's exactly where it's supposed to be!

Left-Right game FAQ

What is the Left-Right gift exchange game?

A story-driven passing game: everyone holds a wrapped gift and passes it left or right on cue as a story is read aloud. Keep what you're holding when the story ends.

Is it the same as a white elephant exchange?

No — no stealing and no choices. It's often named alongside white elephant games, but the mechanics are entirely different.

How long should the story be?

2–4 minutes with 25–40 direction words — about one cue per sentence or two.

Can you play Left-Right with a big group?

It's the best big-group exchange there is: 60+ players work fine since nothing is sequential. In huge rooms, read louder and slower.

What if someone ends up with their own gift?

House rules: either they keep it (their luck), or the room does one final round of "pass it left." Decide before the story starts.
