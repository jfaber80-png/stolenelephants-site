The Best White Elephant Gifts

Ranked by one metric only: how many times real people stole them. Sourced from years of steal-chain reports across Reddit's gifting communities — every entry below survived contact with an actual exchange.

Stolen 7× · Verified

Quick answer

The best white elephant gifts either combine real usefulness with a story or are so absurd they become the night's legend. The worst gift is a boring one nobody steals. This list ranks ideas by how often real players report them being stolen the maximum number of times, sorted by budget.

How this list was built

We combed threads where people report what actually got stolen the maximum number of times — "what's the best gift you've ever gotten," "gifts that will cause a family fight," "low-budget winners." The pattern: gifts that win either combine usefulness with a story, or are so absurd they become the night's legend. Both paths work. Never show up with the middle: a boring gift nobody steals is the only real failure in this game.

The hall of fame (stolen the max, repeatedly)

  1. The booze wreath

    A large wreath with airplane bottles of liquor tied throughout. Reportedly unrivaled at adult parties. Rotate to a hot-cocoa-and-peppermint wreath for alcohol-free rooms.

  2. The walnut $20

    A walnut cracked, emptied, a folded $20 inside, glued shut, wrapped in a jewelry box. Gets opened ironically, then fought over savagely.

  3. The Costco rotisserie chicken

    Yes, really — documented "carnage." Keep it warm, present it with gravity.

  4. Lego adult set

    Botanicals, IDEAS sets, anything display-worthy. "Stolen the max amount of times" is the standard report.

  5. The liquor-filled mason jar

    A gallon jar: airplane bottles, a $5 scratcher stuffed in the middle, candy filling the gaps, bow on top.

  6. Custom photo gift

    A calendar with a coworker's face on Renaissance paintings. One office version — a manager's face on a "Fan Club" tote — was stolen seven times.

  7. The engraved cutting board

    A grandmother's recipe engraved in her handwriting caused a multi-generational battle. Personalization plus utility is a nuclear combination.

  8. Lottery-ticket bundle

    Taped inside a decoy box, inside another box. Scratchers plus cash plus packaging theater: the most reliable steal magnet per dollar spent.

The five archetypes — pick one per party

A healthy pool has a spread. Here's the taxonomy every veteran brings:

  1. The universally wanted

    Blankets, speakers, gift cards, quality consumables. Someone always needs one — these anchor the steal count.

  2. The useful surprise

    Cleaning-supply bucket, quality batteries, a good flashlight, pizza stone. Ignored until opened, then shockingly contested.

  3. The conversation piece

    Googly-eyed portrait, chippendales calendar (family parties only), giant frame. Gets stolen for the story, not the object.

  4. The hidden-value con

    Cash in a walnut, scratcher in a candle, gift card in a decoy box. Rewards the brave early opener.

  5. The regional delicacy

    Homemade pierogi, local hot sauces, family-recipe anything. The one gift people bargain for after the game ends.

What NOT to bring

The graveyard: half-used regifts with visible wear, anything sized to a body (nobody wants to guess), strong perfumes, perishables that can't survive three hours of party, politically pointed novelties, and — the eternal mistake — the "joke" gift with no value beneath the joke when the room expected real gifts. Check your invite's tone; if unsure, bring the office-safe tier.

Gift ideas FAQ

What makes a good white elephant gift?

Universal desirability or a guaranteed reaction — ideally with a story attached. The walnut $20 and the booze wreath are the canonical examples.

What's a good white elephant gift under $20?

Lego small sets, blankets, lottery-ticket bundles, candles with cash hidden inside, Magic 8 Balls, hot-cocoa kits, and quality snacks all outperform their price.

Should the gift be a gag or something good?

Match the invite. When in doubt, bring a genuinely good gift with one absurd flourish — the crowd respects it and it gets stolen anyway.

What should I NOT bring?

Worn regifts, body-size-dependent items, heavy scents, perishables, personal roast gifts at work, and joke gifts with zero utility when the pool expects real gifts.

How much should I spend?

Whatever the invite says — $20 is the family classic, $25–$50 the office norm. Going over budget secretly is a war crime against the game.
